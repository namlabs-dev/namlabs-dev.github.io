Hidden pair

A naked pair is easy to see because two cells show the same two candidates. A hidden pair is the sneaky version: the two digits hide among other candidates.

What is a hidden pair?

Pick a row, column or box and look at where two digits can go. If both digits fit in exactly the same two cells, and nowhere else in the house, those two cells must hold those two digits. So every other candidate in those two cells can be erased.

The cells look crowded, with extra candidates, but those extras are impossible. That is why the pair is "hidden".

Example

In row 1, the digit 5 can only go in R1C1 or R1C9. The digit 6 fits in the same two cells and nowhere else in the row. So one of these cells is 5 and the other is 6.

That means R1C1 and R1C9 cannot be anything else. Erase 1 and 8 from R1C1, and 1, 4 and 8 from R1C9. Both cells are now plain {5, 6}.

How to find hidden pairs

  1. Pick a house with several empty cells and write the candidates.
  2. For each digit, count the cells where it fits. Note the digits with exactly two spots.
  3. If two digits share the very same two cells, you have a hidden pair.
  4. Erase every other candidate from those two cells.
Tip: a hidden pair and a naked pair are two sides of one idea. If the leftover cells of the house make a pair or triple on their own, it can be quicker to find that instead.

Where it fits

If you already know naked pairs, this is the natural next step. After it, learn naked triples, which extend the idea to three cells.
